What drives the cost of a kitchen remodel?

The main cost drivers are the amount of work and the materials selected. Keeping the current layout and replacing finishes is different from moving a sink, changing cabinet locations, or opening a wall. Cabinet construction, countertop material, flooring, fixtures, appliances, and finish details also affect the proposal.

The condition of the existing kitchen matters, too. A contractor may need to address damage, outdated wiring or plumbing, or surfaces that are not ready for new finishes. Those items can be hard to confirm until the existing materials are removed, so ask how hidden conditions are documented and approved before added work begins.

Compare written estimates line by line. Check whether demolition, disposal, labor, materials, delivery, installation, and final cleanup are included, and note any items listed as allowances or excluded from the work. A detailed estimate makes it easier to understand why bids differ without relying on a headline total alone.

What can affect the project timeline?

Timing depends on the scope, the order in which trades need to work, and whether materials arrive when expected. Layout changes and work that requires inspections can add coordination steps. Custom selections or items with longer lead times may also affect when installation can begin.

Before work starts, ask for a schedule that identifies major phases, decision deadlines, and how delays will be communicated. Find out how the kitchen will be protected during construction and whether the household should plan for periods without a sink, cooking appliances, or normal access to the room.

What local rules and site conditions should you check?

Permit needs depend on the work being done, not simply on the fact that the kitchen is being remodeled. Changes involving structural elements, plumbing, or electrical systems may call for permits or inspections. Confirm current requirements with the City of Brookhaven or the relevant local department before work begins.

If the home is part of a neighborhood association or has shared property rules, check whether approvals or work-hour restrictions apply. Ask the contractor who is responsible for confirming permit requirements and scheduling inspections, and make sure that responsibility is clear in writing.

What mistakes should you avoid before hiring?

  • Choosing a bid by total alone without checking whether the scope and materials match.
  • Starting demolition before selections, responsibilities, and change approval are written down.
  • Assuming permits or inspections are unnecessary without checking the project details.
  • Accepting vague language about allowances, cleanup, or work that may be needed behind walls.
  • Making late changes without asking how they affect cost and schedule.
  • Not agreeing on who will be the main contact once work is underway.

A clear contract should describe the work, payment terms, material choices or allowances, schedule expectations, and how changes are handled. If a part of the proposal is unclear, ask for a written explanation before signing.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What work is included in this estimate, and what is excluded?
  • Who will manage the project and be my main contact?
  • Which materials are specified, and which choices are allowances?
  • How do you handle concealed damage or other unexpected work?
  • Who checks permit requirements and arranges inspections?
  • What schedule do you expect, and what could change it?
  • How are change requests priced and approved?
  • What protection and cleanup are included while the work is underway?
