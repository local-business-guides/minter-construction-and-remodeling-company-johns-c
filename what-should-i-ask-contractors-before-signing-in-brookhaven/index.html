What should you ask before signing?

Ask for a clear description of the work, the materials and allowances, a payment schedule tied to progress, and a process for written change orders. Confirm who will manage the job, who is responsible for permits, how the site will be protected, and what happens if the work or schedule changes.

A useful contract gives both sides the same expectations before work starts. If a proposal relies on verbal promises, leaves major items vague, or asks you to approve a change without a written cost and schedule effect, pause and get the details in writing.

What should the project scope cover?

The scope should say what areas are included, what work will be done, and what is excluded. It should identify key materials or clearly explain any allowances, such as a budget set aside for fixtures that you will choose later. Ask who supplies each item and who handles delivery, installation, and disposal.

For a kitchen or bathroom remodel, confirm whether the proposal includes demolition, plumbing or electrical work, finish installation, cleanup, and any needed inspections. The exact scope depends on the home and the plans. Get any assumptions about hidden conditions, such as damage found after demolition, written down before signing.

What should a local professional check before work begins?

A contractor should review the existing space, your goals, access to the work area, and the condition of the parts of the home affected by the project. For a renovation, ask how the contractor will handle conditions that cannot be confirmed until walls, floors, or fixtures are opened.

The contractor should also explain which permits or inspections may apply and who will arrange them. Requirements can depend on the project details, so confirm the plan with the City of Brookhaven or the appropriate local authority rather than assuming every remodel follows the same rules.

What changes the cost and timeline?

Price depends on the size and condition of the work area, the amount of demolition, the materials selected, and how much plumbing, electrical, structural, or finish work is involved. Custom details and work in an occupied home can also affect the labor and coordination required. Ask each contractor to identify allowances and exclusions so you can see what the quoted amount actually covers.

Timing can change with permit or inspection needs, material availability, trade scheduling, discovery of hidden damage, and decisions that are still pending. Ask for a realistic sequence of work, the expected start window, and the events that could move the finish date. No contractor can responsibly promise that an unknown condition will never affect the schedule.

How should you check permits and project responsibilities?

Ask the contractor which permits and inspections may apply to your specific scope, who will submit applications, and whether the contract includes related fees. Check the answer with the City of Brookhaven or the relevant authority, especially when the project changes structural elements, utilities, or the exterior.

Also confirm who coordinates subcontractors, protects floors and furnishings, secures the work area, and removes construction debris. If you live in a community with its own approval process, ask whether you need to obtain that approval before the contractor schedules work.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What work is included, and what is specifically excluded from this proposal?
  • Who will manage the project and be my main contact once work starts?
  • Which permits or inspections may be needed, and who will arrange them?
  • What materials, brands, or allowances are included, and how are selections finalized?
  • How will you document a change in scope, cost, or schedule before doing the added work?
  • What is the payment schedule, and what completed work does each payment cover?
  • How will you protect the home, control dust, and handle daily cleanup and debris?
  • What warranty or follow-up process applies to the work, and can I speak with a recent customer whose project is similar?

Listen for specific answers, not just assurances. A contractor who explains responsibilities and puts the answers in the agreement gives you a better basis for deciding than one who leaves important terms for later.

What mistakes should you avoid, and when should you call a pro?

Avoid choosing on the total alone, signing a blank or incomplete agreement, or treating an estimate as a complete scope. Do not assume that a verbal conversation changes the contract. Keep written copies of the proposal, selections, approvals, and change orders, and make sure both sides agree before additional work begins.

Call a qualified contractor when the project involves several trades, possible structural changes, uncertain existing conditions, or work that may need permits. If you are unsure whether an item is included or how a proposed change affects the home, ask for an explanation before approving the work.
